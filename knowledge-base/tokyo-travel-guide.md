# Tokyo Travel Guide

A quick primer for a first trip to Tokyo, Japan's capital and one of the largest metropolitan areas in the world.

## When to go

| Season | Months | What to expect |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Spring | March to May | Cherry blossoms peak in late March or early April. Mild weather, big crowds. |
| Summer | June to August | Hot and humid, with a rainy season in June. Fireworks and festivals in July and August. |
| Autumn | September to November | Comfortable temperatures and colorful foliage in November. Typhoon risk in September. |
| Winter | December to February | Cold, dry, and mostly sunny. Fewer tourists and clear views of Mount Fuji. |

## Getting there and around

- **Airports:** Narita (NRT) is about 60 km east of the city. Haneda (HND) is closer and usually faster to reach central Tokyo.
- **From the airport:** The Narita Express and Keisei Skyliner serve Narita. The Tokyo Monorail and Keikyu Line serve Haneda.
- **In the city:** Trains and subways run roughly 5:00 to midnight. Get a Suica or PASMO IC card, or add one to your phone wallet, and tap in and out at the gates.
- **Taxis** are clean and reliable but expensive. Doors open automatically, so do not pull the handle.

## Neighborhoods to know

- **Shinjuku:** Major transit hub, skyscrapers, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observation deck, and nightlife in Golden Gai.
- **Shibuya:** The famous scramble crossing, Hachiko statue, and shopping.
- **Asakusa:** Senso-ji temple, Nakamise shopping street, and an older Tokyo feel.
- **Akihabara:** Electronics, anime, and games.
- **Ginza:** Upscale shopping, department stores, and fine dining.
- **Harajuku:** Youth fashion on Takeshita Street and the peaceful Meiji Shrine next door.
- **Tsukiji and Toyosu:** Toyosu hosts the wholesale fish market. Tsukiji's outer market still has great street food.

## Food

- Try sushi, ramen, tempura, tonkatsu, yakitori, and okonomiyaki.
- Convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) sell surprisingly good meals, onigiri, and coffee.
- Department store basement food halls, called depachika, are excellent for takeaway lunches.
- Tipping is not expected anywhere.

## Money and practical tips

- The currency is the Japanese yen (JPY). Cash is still widely used, though cards and IC payments are common in the city.
- 7-Eleven ATMs reliably accept foreign cards.
- Pocket Wi-Fi or an eSIM makes navigation much easier. Google Maps handles train routes and platforms well.
- Trash cans are rare. Carry a small bag for your rubbish.
- Keep left on escalators in Tokyo and let people pass on the right.
- Many temples and traditional restaurants ask you to remove your shoes at the entrance.

## Etiquette

- Speak quietly on trains and keep phones on silent.
- Do not eat while walking in busy areas.
- Bow slightly when greeting or thanking someone.
- Queue in orderly lines at train platforms and shop counters.

## Useful phrases

| Japanese | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Konnichiwa | Hello |
| Arigatou gozaimasu | Thank you |
| Sumimasen | Excuse me / sorry |
| Eigo no menyuu wa arimasu ka? | Do you have an English menu? |
| Ikura desu ka? | How much is it? |

## Day trips

- **Kamakura:** Great Buddha statue and seaside temples, about an hour by train.
- **Nikko:** UNESCO-listed shrines and mountain scenery, about two hours away.
- **Hakone:** Hot springs and Mount Fuji views, reachable in roughly 90 minutes.
