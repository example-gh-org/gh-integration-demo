# How to Travel in Tokyo

Tokyo is one of the world's most exciting cities, blending ancient tradition with cutting-edge modernity. Here is a practical guide to help you make the most of your visit.

## Getting Around

The Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway networks cover virtually every corner of the city. Purchase a Suica or Pasmo IC card at any station — you can tap in and out at turnstiles and even use the card at convenience stores and vending machines. For day trips to Nikko, Kamakura, or Hakone, the JR Pass covers shinkansen and most JR lines.

## Neighbourhoods to Explore

- **Shinjuku** — The beating commercial heart of Tokyo. Don't miss the neon-lit Kabukicho entertainment district and the tranquil Shinjuku Gyoen garden.
- **Shibuya** — Home to the world-famous scramble crossing. The surrounding streets are packed with fashion, food, and the Shibuya Sky observation deck.
- **Asakusa** — Tokyo's most traditional neighbourhood. Sensoji Temple, rickshaws, and dozens of craft and souvenir stalls line Nakamise-dori.
- **Akihabara** — The global capital of electronics, anime, and gaming culture.
- **Yanaka** — A quiet, old-downtown (shitamachi) area that survived wartime bombing. Excellent for aimless wandering and independent cafes.

## Food

Tokyo has more Michelin stars than any city on Earth, but street food and convenience stores are just as remarkable.

- **Ramen** — Each region of Japan has its own style; try rich tonkotsu at Ichiran or light shoyu at one of the old-school shops in Asakusa.
- **Sushi** — For the full omakase experience, book weeks ahead. For a fantastic budget option, try a conveyor-belt (kaiten) restaurant.
- **Izakaya** — Japanese pub-style dining. Order small plates and cold Sapporo beer with friends.
- **Convenience stores** — 7-Eleven, Lawson, and FamilyMart sell freshly made onigiri, sandwiches, and hot foods 24/7 at remarkably high quality.

## Practical Tips

1. **Cash** — Japan remains largely cash-based. Withdraw yen from 7-Bank or Japan Post ATMs, which accept international cards.
2. **Pocket Wi-Fi** — Rent a device at the airport or buy a tourist SIM card for reliable data throughout your trip.
3. **Manners** — Speak quietly on public transport, never eat while walking in a crowd, and always queue. Tipping is not customary and may cause offence.
4. **Language** — English signage is widespread in tourist areas. Download Google Translate with the Japanese language pack for offline use.
5. **Shoes** — Many temples, traditional restaurants, and some homes require you to remove your shoes. Slip-ons make life easier.

## Day Trips

- **Nikko** (2 h by shinkansen) — Ornate Toshogu shrine complex set in cedar forests.
- **Kamakura** (1 h by JR) — The giant bronze Kotoku-in Buddha and scenic coastal hiking trails.
- **Hakone** (1.5 h by Romancecar) — Hot springs, views of Mount Fuji, and the open-air sculpture museum.

Enjoy Tokyo.
